# Channel 2 — Quality Control System

The Quality Control System is the final gate before a Short is approved for publication.

Its purpose is to identify problems in:

- Accuracy
- Storytelling
- Hook quality
- Visual quality
- Audio quality
- Authenticity
- Licensing
- Captions
- Viewer experience
- Platform readiness

A video should not be published simply because it is finished.

---

# 1. Quality Gate

The production pipeline becomes:

IDEA
→ RESEARCH
→ STORY
→ SCRIPT
→ PRODUCTION
→ QUALITY CONTROL
→ APPROVED / REVISE / REJECT
→ PUBLISH
→ ANALYTICS

---

# 2. QC Status

Every completed Short receives one of three statuses.

### APPROVED

The video passes all critical checks and is ready for publication.

### REVISE

The video contains problems that can reasonably be corrected.

The video returns to the appropriate production stage.

### REJECT

The video has a major problem that makes publication inappropriate.

Examples:

- Unsupported factual claims
- Unclear source origin
- Serious licensing uncertainty
- Misleading representation
- Broken story
- Unusable production quality

---

# 3. Critical Fail Conditions

A video should not be approved if it contains:

- Major unverified factual claims presented as fact
- Misleading representation of real events
- Unknown or questionable rights for important media
- False quotations
- Fabricated evidence
- Broken or missing narration
- Major audio distortion
- Unreadable captions
- Severe visual artifacts
- A missing or misleading payoff
- Technical rendering problems

Critical failures must be resolved before publication.

---

# 4. Research Check

For nonfiction:

- Event is sufficiently verified
- Major claims have supporting sources
- Dates are accurate when stated
- Locations are accurate when stated
- People/entities are accurately represented
- Quotes are authentic
- Uncertainty is not presented as certainty
- The script does not exaggerate the evidence

For fiction:

- Story is internally consistent
- Fiction is not intentionally presented as a real event

---

# 5. Hook Check

Evaluate:

- Does attention begin immediately?
- Is the hook relevant to the story?
- Does the hook create curiosity, emotion, or interest?
- Does the video fulfill the promise created by the hook?
- Is the hook misleading?
- Is the opening unnecessarily long?

The strongest hook is not necessarily the loudest hook.

---

# 6. Story Check

Evaluate:

### Context

Can the viewer understand what is happening?

### Progression

Does something meaningfully change as the video progresses?

### Escalation

Does curiosity, emotion, humor, tension, or interest develop?

### Payoff

Does the story deliver what the hook promised?

### Ending

Does the video feel complete?

A Short should not feel like it stopped simply because the runtime ended.

---

# 7. Viewer Experience Check

Ask:

- Would a viewer understand the video without prior knowledge?
- Is the pacing appropriate?
- Is anything confusing?
- Is anything unnecessarily repetitive?
- Does the video feel emotionally empty?
- Does it feel aggressively sold?
- Does it feel obviously AI-generated?
- Is the stimulation excessive?
- Does the video provide a satisfying experience?

The system should avoid optimizing attention at the expense of viewer satisfaction.

---

# 8. Visual Check

Evaluate:

- Resolution
- Sharpness
- Composition
- Vertical framing
- Visual continuity
- Asset relevance
- Color treatment
- Motion quality
- AI artifacts
- Watermarks
- Unwanted logos
- Visual distractions

Important subjects should remain visible on mobile screens.

---

# 9. Authenticity Check

For nonfiction:

- Authentic footage is correctly represented
- Stock footage is not presented as actual event footage
- AI recreations are not presented as authentic evidence
- Images do not contradict narration
- Manipulated visuals do not change the meaning of the event

If authenticity cannot be established, the asset should be reconsidered.

---

# 10. Licensing Check

For every important external asset:

- Source is known
- Original URL is recorded
- License has been reviewed
- Commercial-use permission is understood
- Attribution requirements are understood
- Restrictions are understood

If the rights are unclear, do not assume the asset is safe.

---

# 11. Audio Check

Evaluate:

- Narration clarity
- Voice volume
- Music level
- Sound-effect level
- Background noise
- Distortion
- Clipping
- Sudden volume changes
- Unnatural pauses
- Pronunciation

The narration should remain understandable throughout the video.

---

# 12. Caption Check

Evaluate:

- Spelling
- Grammar
- Timing
- Readability
- Screen positioning
- Caption speed
- Important-word emphasis
- Synchronization with narration

Captions should remain readable on a small mobile screen.

---

# 13. Emotional Check

Determine the intended viewer reaction.

Possible outcomes:

- 😂 Amusement
- 🤯 Shock
- ❤️ Emotion
- 👀 Curiosity
- 😮 Surprise
- 😌 Relief
- 🙏 Gratitude
- 🥹 Warmth
- 😎 Satisfaction

Ask:

"Does the final video actually create the intended emotional experience?"

---

# 14. Anti-AI-Feeling Check

The video should be reviewed for signs that may make it feel low-quality or obviously automated.

Potential problems:

- Generic AI narration
- Repetitive visual patterns
- Unnatural facial movement
- AI artifacts
- Random stock footage
- Excessive captions
- Excessive transitions
- Generic motivational language
- Emotionally empty writing
- Visuals that do not match narration

The goal is not to hide AI use.

The goal is to ensure AI does not replace good storytelling.

---

# 15. Technical Check

Before final export:

- Correct vertical format
- Correct resolution
- Correct frame rate
- Audio present
- No corrupted frames
- No black frames
- No accidental gaps
- No missing assets
- Captions rendered correctly
- Final duration is correct
- File plays correctly from beginning to end

---

Example:

The score should assist decision-making rather than replace judgment.

---

# 17. Minimum Standards

A video should generally satisfy:

### Research

No critical factual problems.

### Hook

Immediate and relevant.

### Story

Clear progression and payoff.

### Visuals

Relevant and watchable.

### Audio

Clear and balanced.

### Captions

Readable and synchronized.

### Authenticity

No misleading representation.

### Technical

No major rendering problems.

---

Before publishing:

[ ] Story classification confirmed

[ ] Research verified where required

[ ] Major claims checked

[ ] Hook reviewed

[ ] Story progression reviewed

[ ] Payoff reviewed

[ ] Visual assets checked

[ ] Asset sources recorded

[ ] Licensing reviewed

[ ] AI-generated visuals reviewed

[ ] Narration reviewed

[ ] Music reviewed

[ ] Sound effects reviewed

[ ] Captions reviewed

[ ] Audio mix reviewed

[ ] Mobile framing reviewed

[ ] Technical render reviewed

[ ] Final video watched from beginning to end

[ ] Publication metadata prepared

---

# 19. Human Review

Automation may identify potential problems.

However, human review may remain necessary for:

- Ambiguous factual claims
- Licensing uncertainty
- Sensitive real-world stories
- Questionable authenticity
- Major emotional decisions
- Final creative judgment

The system should flag uncertainty instead of pretending to know the answer.

---

# 20. Feedback Loop

Quality control should improve over time.

When a published video performs poorly or receives negative viewer feedback, the system should determine whether the problem originated from:

- Story choice
- Hook
- Script
- Pacing
- Voice
- Visuals
- Audio
- Captions
- Payoff
- Audience mismatch

The result should feed back into future production decisions.

---

# 21. Core Principle

The QC system should protect two things simultaneously:

VIEWER TRUST
+
VIEWER EXPERIENCE

A video is not successful simply because it gets attention.

The goal is:

ATTENTION → ENGAGEMENT → EMOTION → PAYOFF → SATISFACTION → TRUST
