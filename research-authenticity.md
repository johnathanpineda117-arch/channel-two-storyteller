# Channel 2 — Research & Authenticity

This system protects Channel 2 from misinformation, misleading storytelling, unreliable sources, copyright problems, and unnecessary AI-generated content.

The objective is to make stories entertaining while maintaining viewer trust.

> **Verification is channel-scoped.** Everything below still applies, but
> *whether* it applies to a given story is read from that story's channel
> policy rather than from one global rule. A channel configured to require
> verification enforces exactly the process described here, at the same
> threshold. A fiction-only channel such as RobloxTales makes no factual claims
> and so requires none of it — which is a property of that channel, not a
> relaxation of this system. See [channels.md](channels.md).

---

## 1. Story Classification

Every story idea must first be classified as one of the following:

### Nonfiction

A story presented as a real event or factual claim.

Examples:

- Real survival incidents
- Historical events
- Real animal encounters
- Verified human-interest stories
- Documented unusual events

Nonfiction stories require research and verification before publication.

---

### Fiction

A story intentionally created for entertainment.

Fiction must not be intentionally presented as a real event.

When necessary, the fictional nature of the story should be made clear to the viewer.

---

### Fiction Inspired by Reality

A fictional story influenced by real-world situations, experiences, or themes.

It must not be presented as a specific real event unless the factual elements have been independently verified.

---

## 2. Nonfiction Research Process

For a nonfiction story:

IDEA
→ IDENTIFY EVENT
→ FIND SOURCES
→ CROSS-CHECK
→ VERIFY CLAIMS
→ IDENTIFY UNCERTAINTY
→ APPROVE STORY
→ SCRIPT

The research process should happen before dramatic storytelling is added.

---

## 3. Source Hierarchy

Sources should be evaluated based on reliability and relevance.

Preferred sources may include:

### Tier 1 — Primary Sources

- Official government records
- Court documents
- Official statements
- Original interviews
- First-hand accounts
- Original scientific publications
- Official organizations

### Tier 2 — Established Secondary Sources

- Reputable news organizations
- Established publications
- Academic institutions
- Professional organizations
- Well-documented reference sources

### Tier 3 — Discovery Sources

- Social media
- Forums
- Aggregators
- User-generated posts
- Unverified websites

Tier 3 sources may help discover a story but should not automatically be treated as proof.

---

## 4. Cross-Verification

Important factual claims should be checked against multiple reliable sources when possible.

The system should distinguish between:

- Confirmed facts
- Strongly supported information
- Reported claims
- Unverified claims
- Conflicting accounts
- Speculation

Uncertainty should not be presented as certainty.

---

## 5. Claim Verification

Before publication, identify the important claims in the script.

Example:

Claim:
"A man survived a bear attack after playing dead."

Verification should determine:

- Did the event actually occur?
- Who was involved?
- When did it happen?
- Where did it happen?
- Was the person actually attacked?
- Was the survival method accurately reported?
- Are there conflicting accounts?
- Is the wording exaggerating what happened?

The final script should remain faithful to the evidence.

---

## 6. Avoiding Sensationalized Misinformation

The channel should not invent or exaggerate:

- Quotes
- Statistics
- Deaths
- Injuries
- Locations
- Dates
- Scientific claims
- Motivations
- Relationships
- Outcomes

Entertainment value should come from the story itself rather than fabricated details.

---

## 7. Fiction Verification

Fiction does not require factual verification because it is intentionally created.

However, the system should still check:

- Internal consistency
- Logical progression
- Story clarity
- Appropriate labeling
- Avoidance of accidental claims that appear factual

---

## 8. Media Authenticity

Visual and audio assets should be evaluated before use.

The system should determine:

- Where the asset came from
- Whether the source is legitimate
- Whether usage is permitted
- Whether attribution is required
- Whether the asset has restrictions
- Whether the asset has been manipulated
- Whether the asset is being represented accurately

A visually impressive asset should not be used simply because it is easy to obtain.

---

## 9. AI-Generated Media

AI-generated visuals may be used when appropriate.

Examples:

- Recreating an impossible scene
- Visualizing fictional stories
- Representing historical environments when authentic footage is unavailable
- Creating transitions or supporting visuals
- Illustrating abstract concepts

AI-generated media should not be intentionally presented as authentic footage of a real event.

When a realistic recreation could reasonably be mistaken for real footage, the production system should consider whether disclosure or contextual clarification is appropriate.

---

## 10. Authentic Footage Priority

When authentic footage meaningfully improves a nonfiction story and legitimate usage is available, authentic footage should generally be preferred.

AI should supplement storytelling rather than automatically replace real-world evidence.

---

## 11. Research Record

Each nonfiction story should eventually maintain a research record containing:

- Story title
- Classification
- Event date
- Event location
- Key people/entities
- Primary claims
- Supporting sources
- Conflicting information
- Uncertainty
- Media sources
- Usage-rights notes
- Verification status
- Research date

---

## 12. Verification Status

Stories should receive a status before production.

### UNVERIFIED

The story has not been sufficiently researched.

Do not produce as nonfiction.

### RESEARCHING

Sources are currently being collected and evaluated.

### PARTIALLY VERIFIED

Some important information is supported, but uncertainty remains.

The story should not be published as established fact until critical claims are resolved.

### VERIFIED

Important claims are sufficiently supported by reliable sources.

The story may proceed to production.

### REJECTED

The story contains major unsupported claims, unreliable information, or cannot be responsibly presented.

Do not publish as nonfiction.

---

## 13. Fiction Status

Fictional stories use:

### FICTION

Clearly identified as fictional when necessary.

### REALITY-INSPIRED

Inspired by real-world situations but not presented as a specific documented event.

---

## 14. Research Quality Control

Before a nonfiction video is approved:

- Major factual claims are supported
- Sources are credible
- Conflicting information has been considered
- The script does not exaggerate evidence
- Quotes are authentic
- Dates and locations are accurate when stated
- Visuals do not falsely represent the event
- AI recreations are not misleadingly presented as real footage

---

## 15. Source Log

The future system should maintain a source log for every nonfiction story.

Example:

| Claim | Source | Source Type | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Event occurred | Source A | Primary | Verified | Confirmed |
| Location | Source B | Secondary | Verified | Matches primary source |
| Exact sequence | Source C | Secondary | Uncertain | Accounts differ |

---

## 16. Core Principle

Entertainment should never require us to sacrifice accuracy.

Channel 2 should aim to make viewers think:

"I enjoyed that."

without making them later think:

"Wait, was any of that even true?"

The standard is:

ENTERTAINMENT + CURIOSITY + EMOTION + TRUST

---

## 17. Future Research Agent

The long-term automation system may assist with:

1. Discovering story candidates
2. Finding relevant sources
3. Extracting factual claims
4. Comparing sources
5. Identifying conflicts
6. Creating research summaries
7. Tracking source URLs
8. Flagging uncertainty
9. Preparing a verification report
10. Passing only approved stories into production

Automation should assist research, not automatically determine truth without review.
