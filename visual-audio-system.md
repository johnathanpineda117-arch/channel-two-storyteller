# Channel 2 — Visual & Audio Asset System

The Visual & Audio Asset System defines how Channel 2 finds, evaluates, tracks, and uses visual and audio assets for Shorts.

The objective is to create visually compelling videos while protecting the channel from unnecessary copyright, licensing, authenticity, and quality problems.

---

# 1. Asset Philosophy

Assets should serve the story.

The system should prioritize:

- Relevance
- Visual quality
- Authenticity
- Legal usability
- Emotional impact
- Story continuity
- Vertical-video suitability

An asset should never be selected simply because it is free.

---

# 2. Asset Categories

The production system may use:

### Video

- Authentic real-world footage
- Licensed stock footage
- Public-domain material where appropriate
- Original footage
- AI-generated footage
- AI-generated recreations

### Images

- Licensed photographs
- Public-domain images where appropriate
- Original images
- AI-generated images
- Historical imagery where usage is permitted

### Audio

- Narration
- Music
- Sound effects
- Environmental sounds
- Original audio

---

# 3. Preferred Asset Order

When appropriate, prioritize:

1. Original footage
2. Legitimately licensed authentic footage
3. Reliable free stock footage
4. Public-domain material
5. AI-generated recreation
6. Other legally usable sources

The correct choice depends on the story.

For example, a real survival story should prioritize authentic evidence when available.

A fictional story may use AI-generated visuals when they improve the story.

---

# 4. Free Asset Sources

Initial production should prioritize legitimate free resources.

Potential sources include:

- Pexels
- Pixabay
- YouTube Audio Library
- Other sources approved after license review

Each source must be evaluated based on its current license and the specific asset being used.

A website being described as "free" or "royalty-free" is not by itself sufficient evidence that an asset is safe for the intended use.

---

# 5. License Verification

Before an asset is approved, the system should attempt to determine:

- Source website
- Asset URL
- Creator/author
- License type
- Commercial-use permission
- Modification permission
- Attribution requirement
- Restrictions
- Date reviewed

The system should preserve the relevant license information when practical.

---

# 6. Asset Record

Each important asset should eventually have a record containing:

- Asset ID
- Asset type
- Description
- Source
- Original URL
- Creator
- License
- Attribution required?
- Commercial use permitted?
- Modification permitted?
- Date checked
- Story using the asset
- Notes

Example:

| Asset ID | Type | Source | License | Attribution | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VID-001 | Video | Pexels | Current source license | No | Approved |
| AUD-001 | Music | YouTube Audio Library | Track-specific | Check | Approved |
| IMG-001 | Image | Pixabay | Current source license | No | Approved |

---

# 7. Authenticity Classification

Every important visual should eventually be classified as:

### AUTHENTIC

Real footage or imagery representing the actual event or subject.

### STOCK

Legitimate stock footage used as supporting imagery.

### RECREATION

A recreated representation of an event.

### AI-GENERATED

Created using generative AI.

### UNKNOWN

Origin or authenticity cannot be confidently established.

Unknown assets should not be used to represent important factual events.

---

# 8. Nonfiction Visual Rules

For nonfiction stories:

- Do not imply stock footage is footage of the actual event.
- Do not present AI recreations as authentic event footage.
- Do not manipulate visuals in a way that changes the meaning of the event.
- Important factual visuals should be verified whenever practical.
- Supporting visuals should not contradict the narration.

When authentic footage is unavailable, the video may use clearly contextualized supporting footage or recreation.

---

# 9. Fiction Visual Rules

Fictional stories may use:

- Stock footage
- Original footage
- AI-generated imagery
- AI-generated video
- Creative visual effects

The visuals should support the fictional nature of the story rather than falsely implying that the story is documented reality.

---

# 10. Visual Quality

Assets should be evaluated for:

- Resolution
- Sharpness
- Lighting
- Composition
- Motion quality
- Vertical crop potential
- Color quality
- Visual consistency
- Relevance

Avoid:

- Extremely low-resolution footage
- Obvious watermarks
- Unwanted logos
- Broken or corrupted media
- Distracting artifacts
- Visually inconsistent sequences

---

# 11. Vertical Composition

Shorts should generally be designed for vertical viewing.

Important subjects should remain visible after vertical cropping.

The production system should evaluate:

- Subject position
- Safe areas
- Caption placement
- Facial visibility
- Important objects
- Background distractions

---

# 12. Visual Pacing

Visual changes may be used to maintain attention.

Possible techniques:

- Rapid cuts
- Slow cinematic shots
- Zooms
- Push-ins
- Reframes
- Before/after comparisons
- Detail shots
- Reaction shots
- Environmental transitions

The number and speed of cuts should depend on the story.

Fast pacing should not become visually exhausting.

---

# 13. Color & Visual Identity

Channel 2 should use color intentionally.

Possible approaches:

- Vibrant colors for energetic content
- Cinematic grading for serious stories
- Natural colors for authentic events
- Softer visuals for peaceful content
- Stronger contrast for suspense

Color should reinforce the emotional direction of the story.

It should not make authentic footage appear misleadingly manipulated.

---

# 14. Music

Music should support:

- Curiosity
- Suspense
- Humor
- Emotion
- Energy
- Relief
- Peace
- Satisfaction

Music should not overpower narration.

The system should track whether attribution is required for each track.

---

# 15. Sound Effects

Sound effects may reinforce:

- Movement
- Impact
- Surprise
- Humor
- Danger
- Transitions
- Emotional moments

Sound effects should be used intentionally.

Avoid adding effects simply to increase stimulation.

---

# 16. Audio Mixing

Narration should remain understandable.

The production system should evaluate:

- Narration clarity
- Music level
- Sound-effect level
- Environmental audio
- Sudden volume changes
- Distortion
- Silence

The goal is immersive audio without overwhelming the viewer.

---

# 17. Asset Sourcing Workflow

The future asset system should follow:

STORY
→ IDENTIFY REQUIRED ASSETS
→ SEARCH APPROVED SOURCES
→ CHECK LICENSE
→ CHECK AUTHENTICITY
→ DOWNLOAD / SAVE REFERENCE
→ RECORD SOURCE
→ QUALITY CHECK
→ APPROVE
→ EDIT

---

# 18. Source Restrictions

The system should not automatically use:

- Random Google images
- Reuploaded videos
- Watermarked content
- Copyrighted clips with unknown permissions
- Content scraped from social media
- Assets with unclear licensing
- Assets whose authenticity cannot be established when authenticity matters

A search result is not automatically a license.

---

# 19. Asset Reuse

Legitimately usable assets may be reused when appropriate.

However, the system should avoid making multiple videos feel visually identical.

Reusable assets should be treated as components rather than entire videos.

---

# 20. Asset Database

The long-term system may maintain a searchable asset database.

Possible fields:

- Asset ID
- Category
- Source
- URL
- License
- Creator
- Attribution
- Authenticity
- Resolution
- Orientation
- Tags
- Emotional tone
- Story compatibility
- Date reviewed
- Usage history

Example tags:

- nature
- animal
- survival
- city
- luxury
- friendship
- family
- funny
- emotional
- peaceful
- transformation
- mystery

---

# 21. Quality Control

Before publication, the system should check:

- All major assets have known sources.
- Licensing information has been recorded when required.
- Important nonfiction visuals are not misleading.
- AI recreations are not presented as authentic footage.
- No obvious watermarks remain.
- Visual quality is acceptable.
- Audio is balanced.
- Music attribution requirements are satisfied.
- Assets support the story.

---

# 22. Future Automation

The eventual agent may assist with:

1. Identifying required visuals
2. Searching approved sources
3. Ranking candidate assets
4. Checking license information
5. Recording asset metadata
6. Downloading approved assets
7. Detecting duplicates
8. Checking visual quality
9. Matching assets to story beats
10. Preparing the editing timeline

Automation should not automatically approve uncertain licensing or authenticity.

---

# 23. Core Principle

The goal is not:

"Find free footage."

The goal is:

"Find the right visual or audio asset that we can legitimately use and confidently place in the story."

QUALITY + AUTHENTICITY + USABILITY + STORY FIT
